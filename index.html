What is Bill 96?

Bill 96 (officially "An Act respecting French, the official and common language of Québec") was adopted by the National Assembly in May 2022. After passage, it became Law 14 and amends the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101). It is being brought into force in stages.

Its stated purpose is to affirm that French is the sole official language of Quebec and the common language of the Quebec nation.

Key Dates

  • May 2022 Bill 96 adopted by the National Assembly
  • June 1, 2025 New regulations for trademarks & product packaging take effect
  • June 1, 2027 Grace period ends for non-compliant products manufactured before June 1, 2025
  • No Grace Period Public signage — must comply immediately

Key Areas of Bill 96

🏛️ Government and Courts
  • Stronger obligation for the civil administration to use French in all internal and external communications.
  • Tighter limits on using English or bilingual documents in dealings with the state.
  • Exceptions apply for: Indigenous peoples, services outside Quebec, tourism, and recent immigrants for a short adjustment period.
💼 Language of Work
  • Expands employees' legal right to work in French.
  • Requires employers to avoid unnecessary demands for knowledge of another language in job postings and conditions of employment.
  • Extends application of the French Charter to smaller businesses — threshold lowered from 50+ employees to 25+ employees.
  • Employees may file complaints if their right to work in French is infringed.
🏪 Business and Commerce
  • More stringent rules on French in commercial signage, advertising, contracts, and product labelling.
  • French must be clearly dominant on signs and marketing materials.
  • New regulations for trademarks and product packaging effective June 1, 2025.
  • Grace period until June 1, 2027 for non-compliant products manufactured before June 1, 2025.
  • No grace period for public signage — immediate compliance required.
  • Contracts with the Quebec government and its agencies must be in French.
🎓 Education
  • Imposes caps and quotas on English-language CEGEP enrollment.
  • Adds French-language course requirements even for many students attending English-language institutions.
  • Students from outside Quebec attending English CEGEPs may face additional language requirements.
⚖️ Enforcement and Sanctions
  • Strengthened powers for the Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) to investigate, issue orders, and enforce compliance.
  • Fines for companies: recurring non-compliance can reach up to tens of thousands of dollars per day.
  • Individual fines scale up for repeat violations.
  • The OQLF can issue compliance orders without going to court first.
